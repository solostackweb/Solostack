## POST COPY — final

```
Same work. Same freelancer. Two prices.

Indian designers charge ₹300–2,000/hr for clients here.
For overseas clients: ₹1,000–3,500/hr.

For two years I called that gap "the market."

Then I looked at my own setup and found something worse.

My premium clients got a proper Notion workspace.
Everyone else got a WhatsApp thread — because building a real
workspace for every client wasn't something I could afford to do.

So I was handing some clients a business and the rest of them
a freelancer. Then blaming the market for what they paid me.

Part of that gap is genuine arbitrage. Budgets abroad are bigger,
and anyone who tells you a nicer invoice closes it completely is
selling you something.

But look at those two ranges again. They overlap.
₹1,000–2,000/hr gets charged on both sides — same country,
same skill, different presentation.

Indian freelancers: what's your gap? Market, or packaging?

I'll go first, in the comments.

#indianfreelancers #solopreneur #freelancelife #clientmanagement
```

## WHY THIS SHOULD PULL COMMENTS

- **The overlap is the hook.** Everyone posts "international pays more." Almost nobody
  notices the published ranges *overlap* at ₹1,000–2,000. That's the fresh observation,
  and it's the part people will quote back at you.
- **It concedes the counter-argument first.** The arbitrage paragraph disarms the
  "well actually, it's just currency" reply — those people now have to engage with the
  overlap instead of correcting you.
- **The confession is specific and unflattering.** "Premium clients got Notion, everyone
  else got WhatsApp" is a real detail from your own stack, and you're the one who looks
  bad in it. That's what stops it reading like content marketing.
- **It ends on a genuine question**, and "I'll go first, in the comments" gives you a
  legitimate reason to seed your own thread — no engagement bait needed.

## RISKS

- Someone will read it as "poor freelancers, it's your fault." The arbitrage paragraph is
  the defusing device — **do not cut it.**
- Be ready for "₹3,500/hr is fantasy for most people." Correct answer: agree, point at the
  low end of the overseas range, don't get defensive.
- **You must actually go first in the comments.** If you don't, the post underdelivers.
