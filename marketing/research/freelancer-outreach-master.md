## What counts as a prospect

This is a candidate pool, not 40 authenticated, qualified, or willing participants. A public portfolio supports an invitation; it does not prove current paid work, a pain point, or purchasing power. Email addresses below were publicly provided for professional contact, not purchased, guessed, or tested for deliverability.

Evidence labels:

- **Page**: a public page was retrieved directly. Its professional claims remain self-reported.
- **Index**: search results supplied the evidence, but direct retrieval failed, was blocked, or returned no usable body. Recheck before sending.
- **Hold**: a specific ambiguity needs resolution before outreach.
- **Previous**: evidence and qualifications are in the original research file.

Invite people who have had paid freelance client work in the last 90 days and personally manage at least part of the client relationship or administration. Distinguish full-time solo freelancers, side-income freelancers, studios/collectives, and educators. Do not ask for confidential client documents to prove qualification.

## Scaling the research

Work toward an initial pool of 100 relevant candidates, expanding in batches based on qualification and replies. Forty are currently listed; sixty remain to be researched to reach that planning target. Do not describe the 100-person pool as complete.

Suggested first-phase outcome: 25–30 completed interviews, beginning with the original 15 exploratory interviews and then ten new participants in the strongest segment. Extend only when additional interviews resolve an open question or bring in a missing cohort. Interview count is a workload target, not a proof threshold.

Suggested initial outreach pace: 8–12 individually researched invitations per working day, with capacity for 2–3 interviews and time for notes. This is a human workload recommendation, not a provider sending limit or predicted response rate. If replies exceed capacity, pause new invitations. If two batches produce few relevant replies, improve the invitation/source rather than merely increasing volume.

For planning only: at a hypothetical 10% completed-interview rate, 30 conversations require 300 invitations; at 20%, 150; at 30%, 100. We have no measured rate yet. Track qualified replies, booked calls, completed calls, and reasons for non-participation separately.

Use multiple channels and sources: public portfolios; moderator-approved invitations through [Freelance India Club](https://www.freelanceindia.club/); the previously researched NCR groups; and opt-in introductions via [Peak Freelance](https://peakfreelance.com/about/) or participants. Avoid having one friendship group or community dominate the sample. No community memberships or paid placements have been purchased.

## Stackivo workflow without development

The following is based on the current local implementation. The live workspace still requires completion of onboarding and a practical integration check. The sender's name, Akshat Jain, was visible in the Stackivo setup form; confirm the sending account is the approved Gmail address before sending.

1. **Initial invitation and replies:** personal Gmail. No dedicated arbitrary cold-email composer has been verified in Stackivo. The app's transactional email support does not establish that capability.
2. **Prospect register:** use a research project/task or contact record only where the live UI supports it with truthful available information. Do not invent billing addresses or create synthetic inbound lead submissions. The existing client schema requires a billing address and limits client notes to 500 characters, so bulk-importing cold prospects as customers should not be assumed to work.
3. **After a positive reply:** confirm paid work in the past 90 days, responsibility for client admin, preferred time, and timezone. Then create a 20-minute meeting in Stackivo. Title: `Freelance work research — [first name]`.
4. **Booking:** current code requires Google Calendar to be connected, supports open-availability or specific slots, and provides a token-based guest confirmation page. Confirmations create a Google Calendar event and Google Meet link. Check the guest flow before relying on it.
5. **Public client brief:** `A 20-minute research conversation about your freelance work and experience. No preparation or product demo. Please avoid sharing confidential client information.`
6. **Private notes:** record ranked problems, recent examples, frequency, workarounds, actual spending, stated budget, urgency, and next steps here. Stackivo has a separate Private notes field with an 8,000-character limit. Keep the public client brief neutral: it can be copied into the calendar event.
7. **After interview:** mark the meeting complete and record the participant ID, cohort, and next agreed action. Summarize patterns by segment, retaining evidence that contradicts the original hypotheses.

Use a simple manual status convention wherever the live app permits it: `Researched → Invited → Replied → Qualified → Booked → Interviewed`, with separate `Declined`, `Not eligible`, and `Hold` outcomes. This is an operating convention, not a claim that these exact native CRM stages exist.

### Current email behavior that matters

Creating a meeting associated with a client automatically attempts an invitation email. That path currently uses the app's `share` sender (`share@stackivo.me`) and sets the owner's email as Reply-To. It does not pass the Gmail send-as option, even though Gmail send-as support exists elsewhere. Therefore, connecting Gmail should not be assumed to make every meeting invitation come from the personal inbox.

Create participant-linked meetings only after they agree to an interview. For any internal booking check, use only the founder's own approved account as the recipient, and label it clearly as a test. Meeting creation success alone does not prove the email arrived, because sending is best-effort. No test booking has been created yet.

## Success criterion

A larger contact list makes recruitment possible; it does not validate Stackivo. The research should tell us which specific freelancers repeatedly experience which problem, what it costs them, what they already do or pay for, and why current alternatives fail. Apply the earlier decision rule within a coherent segment, and keep international, studio, educator, and occasional-freelancer evidence distinguishable.
