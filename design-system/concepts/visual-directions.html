Three ways Stackivo could stop looking like every other SaaS site.

Each direction below is a working hero plus one supporting section, built with real Stackivo copy. They are deliberately far apart — the point is to find out which end of the range you actually want before I rewrite MASTER.md and rebuild seventeen pages.

All three keep the things that already work: semantic tokens, a 12px type floor, four radii, tabular figures for money, and light/dark parity. What changes is palette, type pairing, section rhythm, and the signature visual idea.

What's wrong today

Eleven sections in a row, each one an eyebrow pill, a centred display heading, a subtitle, then a grid of cards. Competent, entirely anonymous.

Who you're selling to

Freelance designers, developers and studios. People who look at a lot of websites and can tell a template on sight.

The unused asset

GST-native, INR-first, built in India for Indian independents. Currently styled as though you were hiding it.

A

Ledger

Trust through craft. Warm paper, real ink, a serif with authority, and numbers set like a proper financial document. The argument: you are asking freelancers to run their money through you, so look like something that takes money seriously. Almost nobody in this category is doing left-aligned editorial — it reads as considered rather than assembled.

Palette

#F6F2EA paper · #EDE7DB raised · #D6CFC0 rule
#7A2E1E accent · #5A564C muted · #14140F ink

Type

Instrument Serif display · Inter UI and body · IBM Plex Mono figures, labels, IDs. Three faces, three jobs, no overlap.

Signature

Hairline rules instead of cards. Content sits on the page, not in floating boxes. Offset paper shadow, never a blur.

Risk

Can drift formal or dated if the serif is over-used. Needs strict discipline: display only, never body.

A · Supporting section

Tax, handled quietly.

The unglamorous parts of running a practice — place of supply, reverse charge, export invoices without GST — are exactly where the software should be doing the thinking. You pick the client; Stackivo picks the treatment.

01 / INTRASTATE

Same state, split tax

Client in Karnataka, you in Karnataka. CGST 9% and SGST 9%, applied separately and shown separately, because that is how the return expects it.

02 / INTERSTATE

Different state, one line

Client in Maharashtra. IGST 18% as a single line. No mental arithmetic, no template you edited last quarter and forgot to change back.

03 / EXPORT

Overseas, zero-rated

Client in Berlin paying in euros. LUT declaration on the invoice, zero-rated supply, FX recorded at the date of issue for your books.

B

Workbench

An instrument, not a brochure. Near-black canvas, a real 1px structural grid, zero drop shadows, and one high-voltage accent used sparingly enough that it still means something. The argument: your buyers are technical and design-literate, and they respect tools that look like tools. This is the closest to where you already are — but it commits, where the current site hedges with soft blur.

Palette

#08090A canvas · #0E1011 surface · #1E2224 hairline
#C6F24E volt · #8A9296 muted · #F2F4F3 fg

Type

Inter at tight tracking (−0.04em) for display · IBM Plex Mono for every label, eyebrow and figure. Two faces doing very different jobs.

Signature

Everything sits on a visible hairline grid. Gaps are 1px lines, not whitespace. Numbers are always mono and always tabular.

Risk

Dark-first sites convert worse for non-technical buyers, and the lime is polarising. Needs a genuinely good light mode too, not an afterthought.

C

Daylight

Warm, human, and unmistakably made here. Cream paper, deep forest green, terracotta accent, generous rounded geometry and a plain-spoken voice. The argument: every competitor is a cold blue imitation of a Bay Area product. Being visibly Indian-made and warm about it is a positioning moat, not a limitation — and it is the friendliest of the three to a non-technical buyer.

Palette

#FBF7F0 cream · #F2EBDF raised · #D4562A terracotta
#12352A forest · #65625A muted · #1A1A17 ink

Type

Bricolage Grotesque display, tight and characterful · Inter body · IBM Plex Mono for money only.

Signature

Soft stacked cards with a flat offset behind them — depth without blur. Full-bleed forest bands to break the cream.

Risk

Warmth can read as "small" to enterprise buyers. Less of a concern for a freelancer product, but it does cap you if you move upmarket.

What I'd suggest, and what I need from you

If it were mine: A · Ledger for marketing, B · Workbench for the app. A product that holds contracts and money should feel considered on the way in and precise once you're inside, and that split is honest rather than inconsistent — the marketing site and the dashboard are doing genuinely different jobs. C is the boldest positioning play and the one a competitor could not copy quickly, but the cream palette fights dense data screens, so it would stay marketing-only too.

Three things worth knowing before you pick:

  • The heroes here use a real invoice, real GST cases and real figures. That is not decoration — the reason your current hero reads as generic is that its floating fragments say nothing specific. Whichever direction wins, the product visuals should show actual Stackivo output.
  • Each direction implies a different voice, and I have written the copy accordingly. Look at the headlines as carefully as the colours; tone will outlast the palette.
  • A mixed answer is fine. "C's palette with A's layout discipline" is a legitimate result, and cheaper than it sounds.
